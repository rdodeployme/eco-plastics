Chapter 01/Opportunity

The opportunity.

Australia recovered about 14% of the plastic that reached end of life in 2023–24. Rigid plastics are the easiest place to lift that.

01 Where to start

Bins, bumpers, pallets.
The easy wins.

Some of the most recyclable plastics there are, and too many of them still end up in landfill.

  • Big and rigidHeavy, solid items. Easy to identify, worth collecting in bulk.
  • One polymer eachA wheelie bin is HDPE. A bumper bar is PP. A pallet is one or the other.
  • Closed loopBin back to bin. Pallet back to pallet.

01 Why now

Three reasons the timing is right.

2021–22

Plastic has to be processed here.

Mixed waste plastics came under export rules on 1 July 2021, and single-polymer plastics on 1 July 2022. Waste plastic can only be exported once it’s been processed into a value-added material, under licence.3

80%

The national target.

The National Waste Policy Action Plan sets a target of an 80% average resource recovery rate from all waste streams by 2030.4

$180

Landfill keeps getting dearer.

The NSW waste levy in metropolitan Sydney is $180.20 a tonne from 1 July 2026. Victoria’s metropolitan rate is $177.19.5,6

01 The impact

Bin back to bin.

The loop is easy to explain because the material doesn’t change. A used bin becomes clean flake, and clean flake can become a bin again.

  1. A used binCollected in Sydney, Canberra or Adelaide.
  2. Clean HDPE flakeTo be stripped, shredded, washed and separated at North Geelong.
  3. A new binOr pipe, pallets and crates.
