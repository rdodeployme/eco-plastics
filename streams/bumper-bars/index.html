02 The streams/02 of 03

Car bumper bars.

PPPolypropylene Resin code 5

The hardest of the three, and the most specialised supply. Smash repairers and wreckers produce them every day.

Polymer
Mostly talc-filled PP/EPDM. Some older bumpers are PU or PC blends.
Density
PP 0.90–0.91 g/cm³; talc-filled grades are heavier8
Difficulty
Hardest. Paint and mixed parts.

02 In and out

Where it comes from. What it becomes.

Comes from

  • Smash repairers
  • Auto wreckers and dismantlers
  • Insurers’ salvage

Comes off

  • Paint
  • Steel brackets and clips
  • Parking sensors
  • Foam energy absorbers (EPP)
  • Labels

Becomes

  • Automotive and industrial mouldings
  • Crates
  • Pallets

02 Its path through the line Proposed

Eight steps, in its own lane.

  1. 01Receive and weighEach load weighed in and logged against its stream.
  2. 02Inspect and sortChecked by stream, and by polymer for pallets.
  3. 03StripBrackets, clips, sensors and foam come off.
  4. 04ShredCut down to flake.
  5. 05WashDirt, labels and residue washed off.
  6. 06Float–sinkAnything heavier than water drops out.
  7. 07DryFlake dried, ready to bag.
  8. 08Bag or pelletBagged as flake, or extruded to pellet.
Talc

Talc-filled PP is denser than plain PP and can behave differently in the float–sink tank, so bumper flake will be tested before a run.

Older bumpers

Some older bumpers are PU or PC blends, not PP. They will be sorted out at inspection.
